Simple Theory

🟢 n-type Semiconductor

When Silicon or Germanium is doped with a pentavalent impurity like Phosphorus, Arsenic or Antimony, the impurity atom has 5 valence electrons.

Four electrons form covalent bonds with neighbouring Silicon atoms. The fifth electron is extra and weakly bound, so it becomes free for conduction.

Majority carrier: Electrons
Minority carrier: Holes

🌸 p-type Semiconductor

When Silicon or Germanium is doped with a trivalent impurity like Boron, Aluminium or Indium, the impurity atom has only 3 valence electrons.

It forms only three covalent bonds. One bond remains incomplete, creating a hole. This hole is available for conduction.

Majority carrier: Holes
Minority carrier: Electrons

Board Exam Answer

n-type semiconductor: It is obtained by doping pure Si or Ge with pentavalent impurity atoms such as P, As or Sb. Four valence electrons of the dopant form covalent bonds with neighbouring Si atoms, while the fifth electron remains weakly bound and becomes free for conduction. Therefore electrons are majority carriers and holes are minority carriers. For n-type semiconductor, ne >> nh.

p-type semiconductor: It is obtained by doping pure Si or Ge with trivalent impurity atoms such as B, Al or In. The dopant forms only three covalent bonds, and the fourth bond remains incomplete, creating a hole. Therefore holes are majority carriers and electrons are minority carriers. For p-type semiconductor, nh >> ne.
