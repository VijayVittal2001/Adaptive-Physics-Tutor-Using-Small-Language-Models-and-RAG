Simple Theory

A p-n junction is formed when a p-type region and an n-type region are created in the same semiconductor crystal.

In the n-type region, electrons are majority carriers. In the p-type region, holes are majority carriers. Because of concentration difference, electrons diffuse from n-side to p-side and holes diffuse from p-side to n-side.

When electrons leave the n-side, positive donor ions remain near the junction. When holes leave the p-side, negative acceptor ions remain near the junction. This region becomes free from mobile charge carriers and is called the depletion region.

The fixed ions create an electric field from n-side to p-side. This field produces drift current opposite to diffusion current. At equilibrium, diffusion current becomes equal to drift current, so net current is zero.

Board Exam Answer

A p-n junction is formed by creating p-type and n-type regions in the same semiconductor crystal. During formation, holes diffuse from p-side to n-side and electrons diffuse from n-side to p-side due to concentration gradient. This gives rise to diffusion current.

When electrons diffuse from n-side to p-side, they leave behind immobile positive donor ions on the n-side. Similarly, when holes diffuse from p-side to n-side, they leave behind immobile negative acceptor ions on the p-side. The region near the junction becomes depleted of free charge carriers and is called the depletion region.

Due to positive ions on n-side and negative ions on p-side, an electric field is set up from n-side to p-side. This produces drift current opposite to diffusion current. At equilibrium, diffusion current equals drift current and no net current flows across the junction.
