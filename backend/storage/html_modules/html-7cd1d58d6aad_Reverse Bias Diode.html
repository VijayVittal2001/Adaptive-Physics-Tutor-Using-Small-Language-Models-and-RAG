Simple Theory

A p-n junction diode is said to be in reverse bias when the p-side is connected to the negative terminal of the battery and the n-side is connected to the positive terminal.

The applied voltage supports the built-in barrier potential. Because of this, the effective barrier height increases and the depletion region becomes wider.

Majority carriers are pulled away from the junction, so they cannot cross the junction easily. Hence, current is almost zero. Only a very small reverse saturation current flows due to minority carriers.

Board Exam Answer

When the p-side of a p-n junction diode is connected to the negative terminal of the battery and the n-side is connected to the positive terminal, the diode is said to be reverse biased.

In reverse bias, the applied voltage is in the same direction as the barrier potential. Hence, the barrier height increases and the depletion region becomes wider. Majority carriers are pulled away from the junction and cannot cross it. Therefore, the diffusion current is greatly reduced.

Only a very small reverse current flows due to minority carriers. This current is almost independent of voltage until breakdown voltage is reached.
