Simple Theory

The V-I characteristics of a diode show how current changes when voltage across the diode is changed.

In forward bias, current is very small at first. After cut-in voltage, current increases rapidly. For silicon diode cut-in voltage is about 0.7 V, and for germanium diode it is about 0.2 V.

In reverse bias, current is very small and almost constant. This is called reverse saturation current. At very high reverse voltage, breakdown occurs and current increases sharply.

Board Exam Answer

The V-I characteristic of a p-n junction diode is the graph between voltage across the diode and current through the diode.

In forward bias, the current is very small until the applied voltage reaches a certain value called threshold voltage or cut-in voltage. After this voltage, the current increases rapidly. The cut-in voltage is about 0.7 V for silicon and 0.2 V for germanium.

In reverse bias, the current is very small and almost independent of applied voltage. This current is called reverse saturation current. At breakdown voltage, reverse current increases sharply.
