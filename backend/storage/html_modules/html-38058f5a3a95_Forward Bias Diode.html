Simple Theory

A p-n junction diode is said to be in forward bias when the p-side is connected to the positive terminal of the battery and the n-side is connected to the negative terminal.

The applied voltage opposes the built-in barrier potential. Because of this, the effective barrier height decreases and the depletion region becomes thinner.

Holes from the p-side and electrons from the n-side move towards the junction. They cross the junction and current flows easily. Hence, a forward biased diode offers low resistance and allows large current.

Board Exam Answer

When the p-side of a p-n junction diode is connected to the positive terminal of the battery and the n-side is connected to the negative terminal, the diode is said to be forward biased.

In forward bias, the applied voltage is opposite to the barrier potential. Hence, the barrier height decreases and the depletion region width becomes smaller. The majority carriers are pushed towards the junction. Holes from p-side and electrons from n-side cross the junction. Thus, a large current flows through the diode.
