Simple Theory

A half-wave rectifier is a circuit that converts AC into pulsating DC using one p-n junction diode.

During the positive half cycle of AC, the diode is forward biased. Current flows through the load resistor and output voltage appears across the load.

During the negative half cycle of AC, the diode is reverse biased. The diode blocks current and output voltage becomes zero.

Since only one half of the AC waveform appears at the output, it is called a half-wave rectifier.

Board Exam Answer

A half-wave rectifier is a circuit used to convert alternating current into pulsating direct current using a p-n junction diode.

During the positive half cycle of AC input, the diode is forward biased and conducts. Current flows through the load resistor RL, so output voltage is obtained across RL.

During the negative half cycle, the diode is reverse biased and does not conduct. Hence no current flows through the load and output voltage is zero.

Thus, only one half cycle of the AC input is obtained at the output. Therefore, the circuit is called a half-wave rectifier.
