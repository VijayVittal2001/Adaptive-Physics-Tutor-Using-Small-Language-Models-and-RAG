Classification of Conductors, Semiconductors and Insulators

Interactive board-exam learning module with visuals, analogies, energy bands and quiz

🚀 Big Idea

All solids do not conduct electricity in the same way. Based on electrical conductivity, materials are classified into three groups:

🟢 Conductors / Metals

Conductivity: Very high

Resistivity: Very low

Example: Copper, Aluminium

Easy current flow

🟡 Semiconductors

Conductivity: Intermediate

Resistivity: Medium range

Example: Silicon, Germanium

Controlled current flow

🔴 Insulators

Conductivity: Very low

Resistivity: Very high

Example: Rubber, Glass

Current blocked

Memory line

Conductor = current flows easily, Semiconductor = current can be controlled, Insulator = current is blocked.

⚡ Visual 1: Conductivity as a Road

Imagine electrons are cars and the material is a road.

🟢 Conductor: Wide highway

Many electrons are free to move, so current flows easily. Hence metals have high conductivity and low resistivity.

🟡 Semiconductor: Adjustable road

Only some electrons can move at room temperature. By adding impurities or giving heat/light, conductivity increases.

Easy Explanation

Think of electrons as students. The valence band is the ground floor, the conduction band is the first floor, and the band gap is the staircase height. If the staircase is too high, students cannot climb. If it is small, some can climb. If there is no staircase, they move freely.

Why semiconductors are special?

Conductors always allow current, insulators block current, but semiconductors can be controlled. That is why they are used in diodes, transistors, ICs, computers, mobiles and solar cells.

🎓 Board Exam Notes

Classification based on conductivity

On the basis of electrical conductivity or resistivity, solids are broadly classified as metals/conductors, semiconductors and insulators.

  • Metals: They have very low resistivity and very high conductivity.
  • Semiconductors: Their resistivity or conductivity is intermediate between metals and insulators.
  • Insulators: They have high resistivity and low conductivity.

Important values

  • Metals: ρ ≈ 10−2 to 10−8 Ω m
  • Semiconductors: ρ ≈ 10−5 to 106 Ω m
  • Insulators: ρ ≈ 1011 to 1019 Ω m

Energy band answer

In conductors, conduction band and valence band overlap or the conduction band is partially filled. In insulators, the energy gap is large, usually greater than 3 eV. In semiconductors, the energy gap is small, usually less than 3 eV, so some electrons can jump to the conduction band at room temperature.

2-mark answer

Solids are classified as conductors, semiconductors and insulators based on conductivity. Conductors have high conductivity, insulators have low conductivity and semiconductors have intermediate conductivity. Examples are copper, silicon and rubber respectively.
